# D1 aligned-rim x-refinement preflight — development evidence

**Decision question.** Would moving the structural and matching pressure y
knot from `.895` to the physical support edge `.900 m` remove the regular-area
pressure change seen when refining the x mesh from 16 to 32 intervals? If so,
one expensive coupled aligned-rim run might discriminate the D1 spatial
question. If the x sensitivity persists, that run has little value.

The independent fixed-structure Q1 oracle kept the same load, clamp,
physical support, other y knots, x mesh family and 8×9 bins. Four
certified PDAS solves cover original/aligned y grids at 16×8 and 32×8.
Every field was also matched to the separately implemented same-mesh Q1
oracle. All active work matrices have full column rank (48/48, 96/96,
42/42 and 84/84). The [raw result](d1_aligned_rim_x_06126ff8_20260928.json)
binds source `06126ff8`, probe SHA-256, oracle SHA-256 and the saved-input
SHA-256. Python 3.14.2, NumPy 2.4.6, SciPy 1.17.1. The
[bounded resource record](d1_aligned_rim_x_resource_06126ff8_20260928.json)
reports 1.03 s and 80.40 MB peak process-tree memory under 180 s / 1 GB.

| 16×8 → 32×8 source-only Q1 change | Original `.895` knot | Rim-aligned `.900` knot |
|---|---:|---:|
| Common physical bin-force L2 | 0.2522% | 0.2554% |
| Rim integrated force | 0.1900% | 0.1920% |
| Physical-support pressure L2 | 7.8236% | 8.8785% |
| Regular-area pressure L2, `0≤y≤.8` | 11.6182% | 11.6018% |
| Rim pressure L2, `.8≤y≤.9` | 6.7602% | 6.3210% |

Original-grid bin and pressure changes reproduce the saved coupled 16→32
comparison to its stated small-motion scalar scope. Rim alignment changes
the near-edge representation, but leaves the regular-area x-refinement
pressure change essentially intact. The aligned grids also have 0.4908%
and 0.5292% FV bin L2 errors at 16 and 32 intervals, respectively,
compared with 0.3344% and 0.4811% on the original grid. The minimum
independently represented gaps in all four cases are between
`−1.79e−8` and `−2.24e−8 m`.

**Decision.** Do not spend the current 180-second coupled development budget
on rim alignment alone. The principal x/front and pressure-space question
remains. This scalar fixed-target result does not qualify deformable-target
contact, moving geometry, mesh-uniform pressure stability or D1 acceptance.
Independent read-only scrutiny confirmed the narrow triage decision. It
also noted that the comparison moves **both** structural and pressure y
knots, changing supported and active counts, and that the two scalar
implementations share stiffness, mass, PDAS and bin utilities despite
assembling contact work separately. The evidence therefore does not isolate
pressure representation or rule out a nonlinear/deformable-target effect.
