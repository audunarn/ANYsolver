# General nodal S3 patch mortar development route

`S3_PATCH_GENERAL_NODAL_PRIMAL_MORTAR_PENALTY_STATIC_DEV_V0` admits an explicit pair of physical S3 V2D shell patches, with one or two triangular faces per side and at least three faces overall. The pressure side is named by the interaction. It works through ordinary von Karman nonlinear static force/displacement control and the multi-control contact path owner. The path has its own `S3_PATCH_GENERAL_NODAL_MULTI_CONTROL_PENALTY_PATH_DEV_V0` identity. These are opt-in development profiles, not C1 engineering acceptance.

Each selected face has a declared physical side and formulation-owned thickness offset. The source and target use disjoint material nodes. Adjacent faces on one side may share nodes; the physical skin positions and work maps at those nodes must agree. The solver retains one physical coordinate triplet per distinct node and one nonnegative P1 numerical pressure coefficient per distinct **source** node. A maximum of four face pairs is admitted, with a finite differentiation budget. Larger patches fail resource admission explicitly; no candidates are dropped.

For each projected source–target overlap, the kernel integrates P1 support, weighted normal gap and pressure Gram blocks over the source **reference-area** measure. It sums those quantities at shared material nodes **before** computing normalized gaps and the penalty energy. Each source face contributes to one pressure coefficient at its shared corner; internal triangulation seams do not create duplicate pressure unknowns. The tangent differentiates the same aggregate energy with respect to unique physical node coordinates. Internal polygon combinatorics can switch as nonmatching triangulation lines cross; a targeted step-refinement check found stable aggregate work through one such switch. True within-surface projected overlap is rejected.

The manager separately reports numerical pressure/weighted gaps and conservative physical lower gap. The latter checks every vertex pair of every declared source–target face pair along the current source face normal. It can reject safe poses when those projected faces do not actually overlap, but cannot hide a crossing. A swept Bézier bound covers the linearized path of all physical corners; unresolved normals or gaps cause cutback or failure. This development bound has not been qualified for folded patches, large director rotation or general self-contact.

The runnable [general_contact_s3_patch_path.py](../examples/general_contact_s3_patch_path.py) presses, slides 50 mm, opens and recontacts two nonmatching two-face S3 patches through one static path. The final route has four source pressure nodes and a version 2 checkpoint. Its 20 mm gap budget and soft 1000 Pa/m penalty are deliberately permissive to exercise the integrated route; neither is a recommended engineering setting. In the measured example, only one pressure DOF is active during pressing/recontact, and the conservative physical lower gap reaches about −6.9 mm. The open phase returns zero contact force. A midpoint checkpoint resumes to the same final displacement within the focused test tolerance.

The local checks cover exact shared-node reference weights, positive Gram rank for a full two-triangle source patch, nonuniform skin work and global force balance. They also retain the earlier single-face weak-gap counterexample: positive normalized P1 gaps can coexist with negative physical gap. Pressure-space convergence, tight independent gap enforcement, arbitrary partial contact, Q4 bilinear/warped surfaces, projected augmentation, large patch search, friction and impact remain required before the plan's first usable handoff. The complete C0–C9 programme remains open.
